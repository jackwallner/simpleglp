# Simple GLP — User Experience & Product Architecture Analysis

This document outlines key suggested improvements for **Simple GLP** to enhance the user experience (UX) and overall product value. The suggestions are rooted directly in the codebase structure and are framed around how a user operates, logs, and experiences their GLP-1 medication journey.

---

## 1. Onboarding & First-Run Experience

The onboarding flow is a user's introduction to Simple GLP. Currently, it gathers the necessary details sequentially but introduces friction and misses opportunities to guide the user.

### A. Presets for Dose and Medication Plans
* **User Operation & Experience Context**: When users set up their plan, they are asked what medication they are taking and must manually type their dose in milligrams (e.g., `0.25`). This requires decimal keyboard input, which is prone to typos (e.g., typing `25` instead of `0.25` or using incorrect separators).
* **Suggested Change**: 
  * Integrate standard dose escalation escalators as presets based on the selected `GLPMedication`.
  * For example, if a user selects **Wegovy**, provide a picker containing the standard step doses: `0.25 mg`, `0.5 mg`, `1.0 mg`, `1.7 mg`, and `2.4 mg`. For **Mounjaro/Zepbound**, offer: `2.5 mg`, `5.0 mg`, `7.5 mg`, `10.0 mg`, `12.5 mg`, and `15.0 mg`.
  * Allow a "Custom" option for compounded formulations. This drastically reduces onboarding input friction.

### B. Pre-Permission Priming for Notifications & HealthKit
* **User Operation & Experience Context**: In `finishOnboarding()`, the app immediately triggers iOS system prompts for `UNUserNotificationCenter` and `HKHealthStore` authorizations in rapid succession. Users are often defensive when presented with system permissions without context, leading to high opt-out rates.
* **Suggested Change**: 
  * Before requesting system permissions, display custom-designed "prep" screens explaining *why* Simple GLP needs this data (e.g., *"We use notifications to keep you on schedule, and HealthKit to correlate your weight loss and sleep with your dose"*).
  * Only trigger the iOS system dialog after the user taps "Continue" on these prep screens.

### C. Active Medication Plan & Dose Steps (Dead Model Code)
* **User Operation & Experience Context**: The codebase defines a `DoseStep` model inside [GLPModels.swift](file:///Users/jackwallner/simpleglp/SimpleGLP/Models/GLPModels.swift#L157-L171) which allows tracking dose changes over time. However, there is no UI in [SettingsView.swift](file:///Users/jackwallner/simpleglp/SimpleGLP/Views/SettingsView.swift) or [OnboardingView.swift](file:///Users/jackwallner/simpleglp/SimpleGLP/Views/OnboardingView.swift) to create or manage these steps.
* **Suggested Change**:
  * Expose a "Dose Schedule" interface under settings. Instead of a single static dose, let users schedule their monthly titration steps in advance (e.g., *"0.25 mg for 4 weeks, then automatic step-up to 0.5 mg"*).
  * This prevents users from having to remember to manually change their plan in settings on titration days.

## 2. Daily Operation & Shot Logging UX

Logging a shot is the core recurring action in the app. Currently, the app assumes a perfect scenario where the user logs the shot at the exact second they take it, which rarely matches real-world behavior.

### A. Retrospective & Past-Date Logging
* **User Operation & Experience Context**: If a user takes their shot on Tuesday morning but forgets to open the app until Wednesday evening, they have only one option: tap the big "I took my shot" button on [HomeView.swift](file:///Users/jackwallner/simpleglp/SimpleGLP/Views/HomeView.swift#L50-L75). This creates a log timestamped for Wednesday evening, which registers as a "late" shot and skews their schedule adherence.
* **Suggested Change**:
  * Add a long-press gesture or secondary "Log Past Shot" button on the main logging button.
  * In the [EditEventSheet](file:///Users/jackwallner/simpleglp/SimpleGLP/Views/LogHubView.swift#L70-L145), allow users to edit the `timestamp` and `doseMg` of individual shots. Currently, they can only edit injection site, notes, and symptoms.
  * If a user overrides a timestamp, recalculate their `scheduleStatus` (using `ScheduleEngine.match`) dynamically so their metrics remain accurate.

### B. Injection Site Rotation Helper
* **User Operation & Experience Context**: Rotating injection sites (e.g., Abdomen, Left Thigh, Right Thigh, Arms) is crucial for GLP-1 medications to minimize local site irritation and ensure optimal absorption. Right now, a user logging a shot has no visual reminder of where they injected last week. They must navigate to `History`, tap their last shot, read the note/site, and manually choose a new one.
* **Suggested Change**:
  * Display a simple human body silhouette or site selector on the **Home** tab showing the last used injection site (e.g., *"Last Shot: Left Thigh"*).
  * Highlight the recommended next site in the rotation sequence, allowing the user to select the site directly *before* tapping the log button, instead of having to find the log afterwards in the `LogHub` tab.

### C. Stepper-Based Symptom Logging Friction
* **User Operation & Experience Context**: To log symptoms (Nausea, Appetite, Food Noise, Wellbeing) in the [EditEventSheet](file:///Users/jackwallner/simpleglp/SimpleGLP/Views/LogHubView.swift#L91-L112), users must tap native SwiftUI `Stepper`s (e.g. `+` and `-` buttons) multiple times for each metric. This is slow and feels clinical.
* **Suggested Change**:
  * Replace the steppers with horizontal custom selection pill grids or emoji sliders (e.g., Nausea: 🟢 None, 🟡 Mild, 🟠 Moderate, 🔴 Severe).
  * Expanding tracked symptoms to include common GLP-1 side effects like *Fatigue*, *Headache*, and *Constipation/Diarrhea* would make the tracker much more medically relevant.

## 3. Insights, Trends, & HealthKit Dashboard

Simple GLP does excellent work importing HealthKit context (weight, active energy, sleep, heart rate, blood glucose) in [HealthKitService.swift](file:///Users/jackwallner/simpleglp/SimpleGLP/Services/HealthKitService.swift), but currently does **nothing** with this data. The Insights screen only shows two basic cards: schedule adherence and timing patterns.

### A. Interactive Health & Metric Charts (Swift Charts)
* **User Operation & Experience Context**: A user tracking their GLP-1 journey wants to see their weight loss progress, how their active energy correlates with their energy levels, and how their dose corresponds to their side effects. Currently, all this health data is locked inside database models and hidden from view.
* **Suggested Change**:
  * Implement an interactive weight trend chart in the **Insights** tab using Swift Charts, overlaying shot events (vertical dashed lines) with their respective doses.
  * Allow users to toggle between charting Weight, Blood Glucose, Sleep, and Steps.

### B. Symptom Peak & Correlation Timeline
* **User Operation & Experience Context**: GLP-1 medications have a half-life of about a week, meaning side effects like Nausea often peak 24–48 hours after the injection, while Food Noise might begin creeping back in on day 6. Users want to understand these personal cycles.
* **Suggested Change**:
  * Display a weekly breakdown chart plotting logged symptoms (Nausea, Food Noise) relative to the number of days since the shot was taken (e.g., *Day +1, Day +2, etc.*).
  * This helps users visually anticipate side effects: *"My nausea typically peaks on Day 2. My appetite returns on Day 6."*

### C. Static Snapshot vs. Active Day Sync
* **User Operation & Experience Context**: Cumulative health metrics (e.g., `stepsToday`, `waterMlToday`, `dietaryEnergyKcalToday`, `proteinGramsToday`) are read at the exact moment the shot is logged. If a user logs a shot at 9:00 AM, their recorded steps and protein intake for that shot day will only represent what they did *before* 9:00 AM, making these fields highly inconsistent.
* **Suggested Change**:
  * Schedule a background task (or run a sync operation when the app opens) to update the `ShotEvent`'s day-specific cumulative metrics once the calendar day has ended.
  * This ensures the shot event contains a representation of the *entire* shot day's health activity, rather than a partial morning snapshot.

## 4. Connectivity, Architecture, & App Extensions

Reliability is paramount for widget and watch extensions. There are a few engineering gaps affecting the user experience.

### A. WatchConnectivity Delegate Registration Window
* **User Experience Context**: If a user logs a shot on their Apple Watch while their iPhone app is killed, the watch message wakes up the iOS app in the background. If the background sync fails, the logged shot won't appear on the phone.
* **Technical Gap**: In [SimpleGLPApp.swift](file:///Users/jackwallner/simpleglp/SimpleGLP/SimpleGLPApp.swift#L60-L66), `PhoneWatchSession.shared.start()` and the message handling closures are initialized inside the `.onAppear` modifier of `SimpleGLPRootContent`. In background launches, the SwiftUI view hierarchy may not load or trigger `.onAppear`, meaning the `WCSession` delegate is never set in time to handle the incoming background message.
* **Suggested Change**:
  * Move `PhoneWatchSession.shared.start()` and the delegate assignments to the `init()` of `SimpleGLPApp` or `AppDelegate.application(_:didFinishLaunchingWithOptions:)`. This guarantees background messages are always captured.

### B. Placeholder Import/Export Screens
* **User Operation & Experience Context**: Under Settings, clicking "Export" or "Import" lands the user on static screens showing only `Export placeholder` or `Import placeholder` (see [SettingsView.swift](file:///Users/jackwallner/simpleglp/SimpleGLP/Views/SettingsView.swift#L176-L188)). The services (`ExportService` and `ImportService`) are written, but completely disconnected from the UI.
* **Suggested Change**:
  * Replace the export placeholder with a share sheet using SwiftUI's `ShareLink` pointing to the CSV URL generated by `ExportService.exportEvents(from:)`.
  * Replace the import placeholder with a file picker using `.fileImporter` to allow the user to select their backup CSV and feed it directly into `ImportService.importEvents(from:into:)`.

### C. Widget Tap Undo Experience
* **User Operation & Experience Context**: The iOS Home Screen widget allows logging a shot with a single tap. While fast, it is very easy to tap by accident.
* **Suggested Change**:
  * When a user logs a shot via the widget, open the app and immediately show the HUD banner message with an "Undo" button (or present a temporary overlay card allowing them to cancel the widget-initiated log).

## 5. Paywall Design & Subscription UX

The proactive alerts are currently guarded by a paywall. The subscription screen (`SimplePaywallView`) is a basic list of buttons.

* **User Experience Context**: A simple list of pricing buttons doesn't build desire or convey the value of the premium subscription.
* **Suggested Change**:
  * Redesign [SimplePaywallView.swift](file:///Users/jackwallner/simpleglp/SimpleGLP/Views/SimplePaywallView.swift) using modern design aesthetics (glassmorphism, clean typography, custom graphics).
  * Introduce a feature comparison grid showcasing exactly what **Simple GLP Pro** unlocks (e.g., *Proactive Alerting, Quiet Hours, Custom Sensitivity, Detailed Trend Charts*).
  * Utilize RevenueCat's dynamic paywall template components if desired, or build a custom premium scrolling layout.
