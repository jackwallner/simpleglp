# Simple GLP — App Store Release Audit

_Audit date: 2026-05-28 · Build 21 · MARKETING_VERSION 1.0.0 (first submission)_

**Overall:** The app is well-built and genuinely close. The purchase flow, privacy
policy, terms, medical disclaimers, and paywall legal compliance are in good shape.
The gaps that matter are: a couple of **submission/approval risks** to clear, and one
**large conversion hole** — the paywall is essentially hidden, so very few users will
ever start the trial. Fixing that is the highest-leverage thing you can do.

Severity legend: 🔴 blocks submission · 🟠 rejection risk · 🟡 conversion/quality · 🟢 polish

---

## 1. Submission blockers (must clear before you can ship)

**🔴 Screenshots are missing.** `fastlane/screenshots/` is empty and there are none
elsewhere in the repo. You cannot submit without at least one 6.9"/6.7" iPhone
screenshot, and **because you ship a watchOS app, Apple requires Apple Watch
screenshots too.** Verify they exist in App Store Connect (they may be uploaded there
directly rather than via fastlane). If not, this is the single thing most likely to
stall you.

**🔴 / verify — RevenueCat + IAP wiring.** Code is solid, but the live submission
depends on ASC/RevenueCat config the repo can't show:
- RevenueCat dashboard offering **`default`** must have all three products attached and
  mapped to an entitlement named **`SimpleGLPPro`** (your code falls back to `pro` and
  to `current`/any offering — good defensive work in `StoreService.swift:128-136` — but
  the dashboard still has to be right or the paywall shows "no packages").
- All three IAPs must be **"Ready to Submit" in ASC and attached to this app version**
  (first IAP submission must be reviewed *with* the build, or you get a 2.1 rejection).

**🔴 / verify — Category not set.** `fastlane/metadata/primary_category.txt` /
`secondary_category.txt` are empty. Confirm a category is set in ASC.
**Recommendation: Health & Fitness as primary.** Avoid "Medical" — it triggers stricter
review and can require regulatory justification you don't want for a v1.0 tracker.

## 2. App Review rejection risks

**🟠 Store name vs in-app brand mismatch.** App Store name is
**"Easy GLP - GLP-1 Shot Tracker"** but the entire app, paywall, legal pages, and copy
say **"Simple GLP"** (`Info.plist` CFBundleDisplayName = "Simple GLP"). This is both a
Guideline 2.3 metadata-consistency flag *and* a trust/conversion problem — users tap
"Easy GLP," open it, and see a different name. **Pick one brand and make it consistent
everywhere.** ("Simple GLP" is what's baked into the binary and all your legal/marketing
— easiest to standardize on that. If "Easy" tested better for ASO, rename the bundle
display name to match.)

**🟠 Trademarked drug names in the keyword field.** `keywords.txt` =
`ozempic,wegovy,mounjaro,zepbound,semaglutide,tirzepatide,…`. Brand trademarks in the
**keyword field** are a classic Guideline 2.3.7 rejection trigger. Note the *generics*
(semaglutide, tirzepatide) and using brand names **in your description** for
nominative/descriptive purposes are far safer than in keywords. Trade-off: these are
your highest-intent search terms, so removing them hurts discoverability. Safer posture:
keep generics + "GLP-1 / weight loss shot / injection tracker" in keywords; carry the
brand names in the description prose. This is the most likely thing a reviewer nitpicks.

**🟡→🟠 Review-prompt sentiment gating.** `ReviewPromptSheet.swift` asks "Enjoying the
app?" → **"Yes"** routes to a direct App Store write-review deep link
(`AppStoreReviewLinks.writeReviewURL`, `?action=write-review`), while **"Not really"**
diverts to a private email form. Filtering by sentiment before sending people to the
store, plus a custom deep-link CTA, is the pattern Apple's ratings policy discourages
("we will disallow custom review prompts"). It's commonly tolerated, but easy to
de-risk: on the "Yes" path call the **native `requestReview()`** (you already have it
wired in `RootTabView.swift:50`) instead of the deep link, and keep the email funnel for
unhappy users. Lower-risk and usually converts similarly.

**🟢 "No analytics" copy vs RevenueCat.** Description and privacy policy say "No
analytics." RevenueCat does transmit purchase data + an anonymous app-user ID
off-device. It's defensible as "App Functionality," and your **privacy policy already
discloses RevenueCat correctly** (`privacy-policy.html` §Purchases) — but to avoid any
contradiction, soften the marketing line to **"No ads. No third-party tracking."** And
make sure the **ASC privacy "nutrition label"** declares **Purchases + Identifiers**
(App Functionality, not linked to identity) — do **not** answer "Data Not Collected,"
which would contradict the SDK and your own policy.

**Not problems (verified good):** medical disclaimers (Settings, terms, privacy),
HealthKit purpose strings (both present per Apple's validation quirk), paywall legal
disclosure (price/renewal/trial/cancel one-liner in `SimplePaywallView.swift:325`,
Terms→EULA + Privacy links + Restore all present), and the privacy manifest (empty
`NSPrivacyCollectedDataTypes` is fine since RevenueCat ships its own manifest).

## 3. Trial conversion — the big opportunity

**🟡 (highest leverage) The paywall is hidden.** It is reachable from exactly two
places: tapping the locked card on the **Patterns** tab, and **Settings → Upgrade to
Pro** (`InsightsView.swift:133/156`, `SettingsView.swift:42`). There is **no paywall at
the end of onboarding and no trial offer in the first session.** For a *weekly* app,
most users may never open Patterns — so most never see the offer, and your trial-start
rate will be a fraction of what it could be. This is the #1 thing to change. Concretely:
- **Add a soft paywall right after onboarding finishes** (`OnboardingView.finishOnboarding`)
  — the highest-converting moment, intent is peak. Make it dismissible (clear "Maybe
  later" / X) so it doesn't gate the core app (stays Apple-compliant).
- **Add a contextual trigger after a value moment** — e.g. after the user logs their
  **first** shot ("Nice — that's shot one. Start a free week of Pro so you never miss
  the next one."). You already fire `glpPositiveMomentForReview`; add a parallel
  trial-offer trigger with frequency caps.
- Onboarding currently **never mentions Pro** — add a one-line tease on the welcome or
  health step.

**🟡 Pro value is too abstract.** Paywall benefits are "proactive alerts," "pattern
insights," "stay on track on autopilot." For a weekly injection, the visceral hook is
**"never miss a dose."** Lead with that, make it concrete ("We watch your timing and
nudge you *before* a dose slips — so one busy week doesn't cost you progress"). The
blurred locked preview in `InsightsView` is well done — reuse that "show what they're
missing" energy on the main paywall.

**🟡 No trust signals on the paywall.** v1.0 has no ratings yet (can't show stars), but
you can add a credibility line — "Private. On-device. No accounts, ever." — and after
you accumulate reviews, social proof.

**🟢 Pricing observations.** Defaults are good: yearly-first, trial badge, auto-computed
"SAVE 72%" (monthly $2.99×12 = $35.88 vs $9.99/yr). Two thoughts: (a) **$9.99/yr may be
underpriced** for the category (GLP companion apps commonly $29–49/yr) — low price lifts
trial→paid but leaves revenue on the table; worth A/B-testing later. (b) **Lifetime
$24.99 vs yearly $9.99** is coherent (2.5× annual) but consider whether lifetime
cannibalizes recurring revenue — many indie apps drop lifetime or price it higher.

**🟢 Reviewer can't find the paywall easily.** Your review notes describe Pro but not
*where* the paywall is. Add to `review_information/notes.txt`: *"To view the
subscription paywall: Settings → 'Upgrade to Pro', or the Patterns tab → tap the locked
card."*

## 7. Concrete fixes (copy-paste ready)

### 7.1 Paywall touchpoint #1 — post-onboarding (highest-converting moment)

`finishOnboarding()` in `OnboardingView.swift` ends by setting
`hasCompletedOnboarding = true`, which swaps the root to `RootTabView`. Instead of
dropping the user straight onto Home, show the paywall once, dismissibly.

Minimal approach — a one-shot flag the root reads:

```swift
// SimpleGLPApp.swift — SimpleGLPRootContent
@AppStorage(GLPStorageKey.hasSeenFirstRunOffer.rawValue, store: GLPAppGroup.userDefaults)
private var hasSeenFirstRunOffer = false
@State private var showFirstRunPaywall = false

// inside RootTabView branch:
RootTabView()
    .sheet(isPresented: $showFirstRunPaywall) {
        SimplePaywallView(paywallImpressionId: "simpleglp_first_run")
            .environmentObject(storeService)
    }
    .onAppear {
        if !hasSeenFirstRunOffer && !storeService.isProUnlocked {
            hasSeenFirstRunOffer = true
            showFirstRunPaywall = true
        }
    }
```

Add `case hasSeenFirstRunOffer = "hasSeenFirstRunOffer"` to `GLPStorageKey`. The paywall
already has a close button (`displayCloseButton`), so it never blocks the core app —
keeps it Apple-compliant.

### 7.2 Paywall touchpoint #2 — after the first shot

`ShotCaptureCoordinator.captureShot` already posts `.glpPositiveMomentForReview` on
success. Add a parallel, capped trial trigger. Suggested rule: fire once, only after the
**first** shot, only if not Pro and not already shown.

```swift
// new notification name in ReviewPromptTracker.swift (or a dedicated file)
static let glpFirstShotLogged = Notification.Name("com.jackwallner.glp.firstShotLogged")

// RootTabView.swift — observe it, gate, then present SimplePaywallView once
.onReceive(NotificationCenter.default.publisher(for: .glpFirstShotLogged)) { _ in
    guard !store.isProUnlocked, !hasSeenFirstShotOffer else { return }
    hasSeenFirstShotOffer = true
    showTrialOffer = true
}
```

Copy for the moment: **"That's shot one. Start a free week of Pro so a busy week never
costs you a dose."**

> Don't stack #1 and #2 in the same session — if the first-run paywall already showed,
> suppress the first-shot one. One ask per session, max.

### 7.3 Review-prompt de-risk

In `ReviewPromptSheet.reviewPitchContent`, the "Rate on the App Store" button currently
opens the deep link directly. Swap the happy path to the native API:

```swift
// instead of UIApplication.shared.open(AppStoreReviewLinks.writeReviewURL)
ReviewPromptTracker.markShown()
finish(.enjoyedMaybeLater)   // RootTabView's onDismiss already calls requestReview()
```

Keep the `writeReviewURL` deep link only for an explicit, unconditional "Rate Simple
GLP" row in Settings (user-initiated CTAs are fine). The "Not really" → email funnel can
stay.

### 7.4 Paywall benefit copy rewrite

Replace the `benefits` array in `SimplePaywallView.swift` with dose-centric, concrete
value:

```swift
private let benefits: [(icon: String, title: String, detail: String)] = [
    ("bell.badge.fill",
     "Never miss a dose",

]
```

## 8. ASO / discovery notes

- **Name (29/30 chars):** if standardizing on "Simple GLP," a strong store name is
  `Simple GLP – GLP-1 Shot Tracker` (30 chars). Keep the keyword tail; just fix the
  brand word so it matches the binary.

  benefit term is `Weekly Shot Log & Reminder` (26).

  2. Home Screen widget + Watch — **"From your wrist or Home Screen."**
